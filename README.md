# Meesho Interactive Prototype

## [Open the working prototype](https://iron-sngh.github.io/Meesho_interactive/)

This prototype explores how Meesho can make the first shopping journey easier to **discover, evaluate and trust** - especially for users who are still deciding whether Meesho is right for a particular need.

Instead of treating discovery, trust and purchase confidence as separate problems, the experience connects them into one journey:

**Context → Intent → Relevant Products → Trust → Proof → Order #1 → Next Mission**

The prototype is fully clickable and is designed around five different shopping personas. Each persona demonstrates a different part of the product strategy from our submission.

---

## Personas

### 1. Rohit - Bihar / Chhath Festival Shopper

**Context:** A shopper in Bihar preparing for Chhath and looking across multiple festive needs.

A generic homepage may not reflect the reason Rohit has opened the app. For a high-intent seasonal session, region and festival context can provide a better starting point.

The Chhath experience reorganises the homepage around the mission - festive fashion, gifting, home preparation and useful essentials - while keeping the underlying trust system consistent.

**Journey**

**Chhath Homepage → Festive Mission → Relevant Products → Trusted PDP → Cart / Checkout**

**What this persona demonstrates**
- Region + season as a lightweight cold-start signal
- Occasion-led rather than feed-led discovery
- Festive collections and mission-based merchandising
- Trust-qualified products within the seasonal journey
- Basket completion across related needs
- The same Mall, MTrusted and PDP trust architecture regardless of region

### 2. Nayan - Assam / Festive Context Shopper

**Context:** Shopper in Assam opening Meesho during a seasonal or festive shopping period.

This persona demonstrates how Meesho can use **region, language and season as initial context** without permanently locking the user into demographic assumptions.

The first session starts with contextual content such as festive picks, utility needs and products trending within the regional experience. As the shopper interacts, real behaviour should increasingly drive personalisation.

**Journey**

**Assam Homepage → Mission Choice → Regional / Seasonal Products → Mall or MTrusted → Trusted PDP**

**What this persona demonstrates**
- Region + language + season as first-session context
- “What do you need today?” mission selection
- Festive and seasonal merchandising
- Regional discovery connected to universal trust systems
- Behaviour-led personalisation taking over after initial interaction

> The regional merchandising shown in the prototype is illustrative and demonstrates the personalisation logic rather than actual regional demand data.

### 3. Kabir - Meesho Mall / Brand-Conscious Grooming Shopper

**Context:** Shopper exploring grooming products who is more comfortable buying recognised brands.

For Kabir, the key uncertainty is not simply whether a seller is reliable - it is whether the product is coming from a brand/source he recognises.

The prototype therefore treats **Meesho Mall as a dedicated brand and provenance destination**, not just another badge.

**Journey**

**Meesho Mall → Shop by Brand / Grooming Need → Branded Product → Proof-First PDP → Purchase**

**What this persona demonstrates**
- Branded assortment organised into one recognisable destination
- Shop-by-brand and shop-by-need discovery
- Clearer source and provenance confidence
- Value discovery without removing the shopper’s choice
- A consistent handoff from Mall into the same PDP evidence system

> **Meesho Mall answers:** “Is this the brand/source I trust?”

### 4. Vivek - MTrusted / Seller Confidence Shopper

**Context:** Shopper interested in a product but unsure whether an unfamiliar seller or listing can be trusted.

Instead of asking the shopper to interpret scattered marketplace signals, the prototype makes MTrusted explainable.

The experience shows **why** a listing deserves stronger confidence through evidence such as seller history, fulfilment reliability, issue outcomes and listing completeness.

**Journey**

**MTrusted Destination → Why MTrusted? → Seller Evidence → Qualified Product → PDP**

**What this persona demonstrates**
- Explainable MTrusted rather than a trust badge without context
- Seller × category performance
- Reliable fulfilment
- Product issue signals
- Listing completeness and product-information quality
- Dedicated seller profile
- MTrusted visibility across discovery and PDP flows

> **MTrusted answers:** “Is this seller/listing reliably good?”

### 5. Aarav - College Style Starter

**Context:** College-going, value-conscious shopper looking for affordable everyday fashion.

Aarav does not necessarily arrive knowing exactly what product or seller to choose. The current feed-first experience can make him do too much of the discovery work himself.

His journey starts with a **mission-guided homepage** where the experience helps narrow the shopping intent through categories such as college, casual and everyday style.

**Journey**

**Guided Home → Men’s Fashion → Product Detail Page → Seller & Review Proof → Complete the Look**

**What this persona demonstrates**
- Mission-guided discovery instead of an endless generic feed
- “Your Style Edit” and category-led entry points
- Relevant product recommendations based on shopping intent
- MTrusted and Meesho Mall as distinct trust destinations
- Proof-first PDP before purchase
- Complete-the-Mission bundles and similar-style discovery after trust is established

## Key Product Ideas Demonstrated

### Mission-Guided Discovery

The prototype moves from **feed-first discovery to mission-first discovery**.

Rather than asking a cold-start shopper to scroll until something feels relevant, the experience uses lightweight context and optional category/mission choices to reach useful products faster.

---

### Meesho Mall and MTrusted as Different Trust Systems

A key idea in the submission is that these two surfaces should answer different shopper questions:

- **Meesho Mall = Brand / provenance trust**
- **MTrusted = Seller + marketplace-quality trust**

The prototype keeps these destinations visually and functionally distinct instead of presenting them as interchangeable trust badges.

---

### Proof-First Product Detail Page

The PDP is designed to make a listing **judgeable before asking the shopper to trust it**.

It includes:

- Category-specific product information
- Clear selected-variant pricing
- Dedicated seller credibility
- Summarised review intelligence
- Verified buyer photos and videos
- Verified Purchase signals
- Delivery estimate
- Return / replacement visibility
- Refund and transaction certainty

---

### Review Intelligence

Instead of expecting shoppers to read dozens of reviews, the prototype summarises recurring buyer themes such as:

**Fit · Fabric · Colour · Quality**

The summary remains connected to real buyer reviews and verified UGC so the shopper can inspect the underlying evidence.

---

### Seller Credibility

Seller trust is brought directly into the PDP through signals such as:

- Seller × category performance
- Reliable fulfilment
- Low issue incidence
- Listing completeness

The shopper can then open a dedicated seller profile when more evidence is needed.

---

### Complete the Mission

Once the first SKU earns consideration, the experience does not return the shopper to an endless feed.

The prototype explores:

- **Complete Your Look**
- Similar / adjacent styles
- Mission-based bundles
- Share & Save / group purchasing

The goal is to turn product confidence into the **next useful decision**, rather than simply more scrolling.

---

## How to explore it

Choose a persona from the left-hand panel and interact directly with the phone.

The major homepage modules, category cards, products, Mall / MTrusted surfaces, seller section, review section, buyer UGC, cart and checkout are all cross-linked so the prototype can be presented as an end-to-end journey.
